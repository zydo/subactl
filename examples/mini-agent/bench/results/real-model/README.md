# The check with a real model

The design asks that what the scripted brain shows be checked with a real
model. Here DeepSeek (`deepseek-v4-pro`, through its Anthropic-compatible
endpoint) is the lead and every worker, on the radix task, and does the work
itself: it reads the failures, edits the code and runs the checks.

- Scenario `scenarios/radix-real.yaml`: eight packages, one worker each, all
  started at once; the harness kills its subagents' pids at the usage limit
  (G4); dev servers are left running (G2); one worker in four runs its
  checks with unbounded parallelism (G6). No external interference.
- Two runs per group, on 2026-11-17, on the physical machine of round 3
  (x86-64, 12GiB chamber). Group A without subactl; group B with static
  envelopes (lead 9GiB, workers 3500MiB, fixers 1GiB). Group A's run 3 was
  added on 2026-11-18 (round 5, commit `f9a9f6b`) for its per-second
  series, which runs 1 and 2 lost (see below).
- `report.md` is generated by `bench report`. Its hypothesis verdicts read
  "undecided" because this check has no group C; the question here is only
  whether H1 and H2 hold with a real model.

## Result

| group | run | packages done | processes left | memory they held | chamber cap hit | time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 1 | 8/8 | 153 | 4.25GiB | 3938 times | 2.8 min |
| A | 2 | 8/8 | 84 | 1.66GiB | 78563 times | 7.6 min |
| A | 3 | 8/8 | 141 | 2.94GiB | 9432 times | 16.7 min |
| B | 1 | 8/8 | 0 | 0.21GiB | never | 12.6 min |
| B | 2 | 8/8 | 0 | 0.22GiB | never | 14.6 min |

What the scripted runs show holds with a real model. Without subactl the
workers leave their dev servers and browsers behind (storybook and esbuild,
up to 4.25GiB), and the chamber runs out of memory in all three runs. With
subactl nothing is left and the chamber's limit is never reached; four
workers per run went over their own limit and were ended (413), and their
packages were done again.

## How far this reaches

- **The leftovers in group A do not come from the usage-limit kill.** In
  run 1 the eight workers finished before the limit at three minutes; what
  was left are the servers they started and never stopped. In run 2 the
  limit fell mid-run and killed four workers, and what they had started was
  left too. A real model leaves processes behind on its own; the scripted
  brain had to be told to (G2).
- **Group A was faster** because nothing held it back: eight workers at
  once, each with all the memory it wanted, at the price of a full chamber.
  Under subactl two or three workers fit in the lead's 9GiB at a time.
- **Three runs of A, two of B, and eight packages**, to keep the cost of
  the model calls small (a run of A, with nine agents calling the model,
  cost about ¥8). The direction is the same in all five runs and the same as
  in the scripted rounds; the numbers are not precise estimates.
- **Group A's per-second series exists for run 3 only**: runs 1 and 2
  were overwritten by group B's output in the same directory, a mistake in
  the runner script. Their summaries were recovered from the runner's log
  and are complete. Run 3 was made to have the series in the charts.
- **A fourth run of A is void** (`void-A-run/`): the model account's
  balance ran low during it, DeepSeek capped its concurrency, and the lead
  gave up after 9.3 minutes with nothing done. It measures the account,
  not subactl, and is not counted. Run 3 took 16.7 minutes against 2.8
  and 7.6: the model answered more slowly that night.
- The first scenario put the limit at eight minutes; a trial showed the
  real workers in group A finish in about five, so it was moved to three.
